COMPUTATIONAL SOCIAL SCIENCE · KAIST

Jiyoung Mo

I study how digital platforms and AI reshape journalism, political information, and democratic life.

I am an M.S. student in Computational Social Science at KAIST. My interests center on how platforms shape news and political communication: how information is produced and circulated, how people assess its credibility, and how AI-generated content, automation, and coordinated activity may affect public discussion and trust. Over the longer term, I hope to study how AI can support inclusive civic participation and collective decision-making.

02 / HOW I GOT HERE

Journey

My path to computational social science began with practical questions about how information is produced, circulated, and trusted.

01

Inside the newsroom

As an intern journalist at Yonhap Infomax, I worked across reporting and a next-generation media project involving social media strategy and short-form news. I saw the tension between journalistic values and the platform and market pressures shaping news production.

02

Political communication in practice

Working on strategy for South Korea’s 22nd general election in a National Assembly office, I encountered framing, policy, candidate analysis, and digital media strategy as practical parts of political communication. This raised questions for me about whose voices platforms amplify and how power travels through public discussion.

03

Investigative reporting and evidence

At KBS’s Current Affairs Planning Window, I worked on fact-checking, public opinion research, field reporting, and broadcast materials. The experience deepened my respect for rigorous journalism and made clear how difficult it can be to establish facts in a complex information environment.

Together, these experiences led me toward an interdisciplinary approach: connecting political communication and technological change with the analytical tools of computational social science.
